## 2. Bitcoin

### Bull

1. **The thesis needs no roadmap.** BTC's proposition — a fixed-supply, no-counterparty monetary
   asset — requires nobody to ship anything. This is the only one of the three with no execution
   dependency, no foundation, no management, no loan book. In a 1–5 year window that is worth more
   than it sounds.
2. **Distribution is built and it is large.** US spot ETFs hold roughly **610,600 BTC** on
   ~**$51.8bn** of cumulative net inflows. Projections of $180–220bn ETF AUM by end-2026 assume
   regulatory progress, but the *plumbing* — desks, custody, advisor access — already exists and does
   not get un-built by a drawdown.
3. **The debasement bid has a mechanism, not just a slogan.** Governments and companies were set to
   borrow **~$29trn in 2026**; Bitwise Europe's sovereign-default model produces an illustrative
   **$224,000** fair value from exactly that. Illustrative, not a target — but the causal chain
   (sovereign borrowing → currency debasement → demand for a non-sovereign asset) is legible.
4. **Regulatory codification is live and near-dated.** The **CLARITY Act** would settle
   commodity-vs-security status and license exchange infrastructure. Kalshi odds of passage before
   April 2027 moved to **52% from 33% in a week**; **15 September 2026** is the next Senate test.
   Analysts tie $200k scenarios to passage.
5. **The next halving lands ~April 2028 — inside your window.** And cycle analysis (Benjamin Cowen)
   points to a **bottom around October 2026**, i.e. potentially before you finish buying.
6. **Portugal's tax code rewards exactly your holding period** — see §5. This is not a small edge.

### Bear

1. **The cycle may not be done.** Prior cycles lost 77%+. Cowen's point is uncomfortable for
   bulls *and* bears: BTC "topped within one week of when it historically tops", which argues the
   cycle is intact — and an intact cycle means the drawdown has further to run before October.
2. **A single forced seller holds 3.8% of all Bitcoin.** Strategy held **818,334 BTC** as of late
   April 2026 at an average entry of **$75,537** — i.e. **underwater at today's price**. If the board
   stops authorising purchases, the marginal corporate buyer disappears; if leverage forces sales,
   the overhang becomes supply. Other treasury companies would follow.
3. **"High-for-longer" is structurally hostile.** A non-yielding asset competes with Treasuries that
   pay. This is not sentiment — it is the discount rate.
4. **ETF flows are two-way.** The same rails that delivered $51.8bn in delivered **$12bn out** in
   three months. Institutional access is not the same as institutional conviction.
5. **CLARITY can fail.** It has already hit a wall once; a 52% probability is a coin flip.

### What would change my mind
Bear-confirming: a Strategy forced-sale disclosure, or sustained ETF outflows through the October
window. Bull-confirming: CLARITY passing the Senate, and ETF flows turning net-positive for a full
quarter.

## 3. Ethereum

### Bull

1. **It is the settlement layer for the tokenization wave in your other memo.** Ethereum hosts
   **over 75% of tokenized RWAs** and **over 60% of global stablecoin supply**; a separate July 2026
   count puts it at **47.9% of RWA value** (the two figures use different definitions — treat the
   *dominance*, not the decimal, as the fact). The tokenized-asset market is **~$65bn, +44% YTD**,
   driven by tokenized Treasuries. BlackRock's **BUIDL** sits at **$2.8bn** in Q2 2026, on Ethereum
   first. **If the tokenization thesis I sent you this morning is right, ETH is the levered
   expression of it** — and NDAQ/ICE/DB1.DE are the conservative one.
2. **Supply is locked and shrinking in float terms.** ~**30% of all ETH is staked**.
3. **The Amazon-2001 framing has teeth.** Standard Chartered's comparison — "stock crashed, but
   business metrics improved" — is the correct *shape* of argument for an asset down 67% while its
   RWA and stablecoin share held. StanChart targets **$7,500**.
4. **Two upgrades inside 12 months**: Glamsterdam (H1 2026) and Hegotá (H2 2026).

### Bear — and this is the more serious of the two bear cases

1. **A value-capture design problem, not a price problem.** **Fusaka (activated 3 Dec 2025)** cut
   blob fees. Lower fees mean **less ETH burned**. So L2 activity can grow — the thing bulls point
   to — while **ETH itself stays net inflationary** unless volume scales enough to compensate. Read
   that twice: *the network can succeed while the token does not.* Bitcoin has no equivalent flaw,
   and this is the crux of my ranking.
2. **The Foundation is in visible distress.** **54 positions eliminated**, **2026 budget cut 40%**,
   the Protocol Security and Engineering team **shut**, the Client Incentive Program **expired**, and
   **at least eight senior staff lost in 2026**. Vitalik Buterin sold millions of dollars of ETH in
   early 2026. You would be underwriting a roadmap while its steward shrinks.
3. **Institutional disappointment is measurable.** ~$11.6bn cumulative ETF inflows by early April
   2026 — a fifth of Bitcoin's — then a **17-day record outflow streak** and net outflows by end-July.
4. **Analyst dispersion is an admission of ignorance.** Citi **$3,175** vs StanChart **$7,500** on
   the same asset in the same year. Nobody has a model that clears.

### What would change my mind
Bull-confirming: net ETH issuance turning negative again post-Glamsterdam **with** L2 volume up, and
Foundation stabilisation (senior hires, budget restored). Bear-confirming: another 40%-scale budget
cut, or a Glamsterdam slip, or RWA share ceding materially to Solana or a permissioned chain — the
last one is the real killer, because it breaks the only bull argument I actually rate.

## 4. NEXO

Note first: **NEXO is not the same kind of thing as BTC or ETH.** It is a claim on a private
company's economics, priced like a token, with none of the disclosure of a listed equity.

### Bull

1. **The most equity-like distribution in CeFi.** Eligible holders receive **annual dividends of at
   least 30% of Nexo's net profit** — a genuine earnings claim, not a governance gesture.
2. **Buybacks are revenue-funded and locked.** Nexo buys NEXO on the open market with platform
   revenue and locks it in the Investor Protection Reserve for **≥12 months**.
3. **Clean float.** **1bn tokens, fully circulating** — no vesting, no unlock cliff, no FDV overhang.
   Rare, and worth crediting.
4. **The regulatory arc is improving.** Relaunched in the **US on 16 Feb 2026** via **Bakkt** for
   compliant trading and custody, with certain products through a third-party SEC-registered adviser
   — three years after the 2022 exit. EU service runs through two **BaFin**-regulated German
   partners (Tangany, DLT Finance). Self-reported **~$11bn AUM** and **$371bn** of transactions since
   2018.
5. **Utility is thickening**: loyalty tiers (Base→Platinum), token-weighted governance live since
   Q1 2026, and better LTV terms when pledging NEXO than BTC or ETH.

### Bear

1. **No user-verifiable Proof of Reserves.** The Moore arrangement is a **third-party attestation**,
   not a Merkle-tree PoR a user can check — unlike Kraken. The prior Armanino attestation ended in
   2023. **The $11bn AUM is self-reported and updated quarterly.** For a business whose entire
   proposition is "trust us with custody", that gap is the whole argument.
2. **Celsius and BlockFi are the base rate.** Depositor funds sit in an institutional lending book.
   A cluster of borrower defaults in a severe bear market impairs withdrawals. We are *in* a severe
   bear market.
3. **Liquidity is thin against an ~$880m cap** — exit is not guaranteed at the screen price.
4. **Regulatory history is real**: a **$500,000 California penalty** for past unlicensed lending, and
   the EU structure is *partners' licences*, not Nexo's own MiCAR licence.
5. **It is a levered bet on the same cycle, plus company risk.** Nexo's revenue depends on crypto
   prices and lending demand. Pairing NEXO with BTC/ETH adds no diversification — it adds beta and a
   counterparty.
6. **Tax may be the quiet killer** (§5): a token paying profit-linked dividends is the profile most
   likely to be classified as a **security** in Portugal, which would **forfeit the 365-day
   exemption** that makes the other two attractive. Unconfirmed — and that uncertainty is itself a
   cost.

### Verdict
**Pass on the token.** You would be taking equity-like risk without equity-like rights, disclosure,
liquidity or (possibly) tax treatment. If you ever want Nexo, want it as a *platform you use
carefully*, never as an asset you hold. And note the recursion: NEXO's dividend is funded by the
same lending book that is the risk.

## 5. The tax structure decides your after-tax return — settle this before you buy

Portugal, as reported for 2026 (**confirm every line with your accountant — this drives the whole
decision**):

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Crypto **not classified as a security**, held **≥365 days** | **Exempt** (Anexo G1, Quadro 7) |
| Same, held **<365 days** | **28%** flat (Anexo G, Quadro 18A), option to aggregate |
| **Staking / lending income** (Category E) | **28%** — and **no 365-day exemption**, ever |
| Mining / validation (Category B) | Progressive rates |

Three consequences that should change what you actually do:

1. **Your 1–5 year horizon and the 365-day rule are a perfect match.** Buy, hold past 365 days, do
   nothing. This is a rare case where the tax code pays you for the behaviour you already have.
2. **Yield destroys the edge.** Putting BTC/ETH on a platform to "earn" converts exempt long-term
   gains into **28% Category E income** — *and* adds the counterparty risk of §4. A 6% yield taxed at
   28% while risking 100% of principal is a bad trade twice over.
3. **The wrapper is not tax-neutral.** Spot crypto you custody yourself and a **physically-backed
   ETP on Xetra are different instruments** — an ETP is a security, so the non-security 365-day
   exemption plausibly does not reach it. Same exposure, potentially very different after-tax
   outcome. **Ask your accountant which wrapper before choosing the broker.** This one question is
   worth more than any price forecast in this memo.

## 6. The bet

**Bitcoin**, as the core. Ethereum as a smaller satellite. NEXO not at all.

**Why BTC over ETH**, in one line: *ETH's bear case includes a design flaw where the network can win
while the token doesn't; BTC's bear case is price, positioning and a forced seller.* Cyclical damage
recovers; a value-capture problem may not. Add no execution dependency, no foundation payroll, the
deeper institutional rail, a halving inside the window, and the same clean tax treatment — and the
risk-adjusted answer is not close.

**The honest tension:** if I were maximising raw expected return rather than survivability, **ETH is
the higher-upside choice** — down 67% with its RWA and stablecoin dominance intact, and it is the
levered expression of the tokenization thesis I sent you this morning. I am not picking it as the
core because the thing that would make it work (fee capture) is the thing management-by-committee has
just made *harder*, and because the Foundation cutting 40% of its budget is not a footnote. A
satellite position respects both facts.

### Kill triggers — write these down now, while it's cheap to be honest

- **BTC**: Strategy discloses a forced sale, **or** ETF outflows persist through Q4 2026 → stop
  adding, do not average down.
- **ETH**: net issuance still positive after Glamsterdam **with** L2 volume up (the design flaw
  confirmed), **or** RWA share cedes materially to another chain → exit the satellite, no reprieve.
- **Both**: if the sleeve exceeds ~8% of the book on appreciation, trim to 5% — **after** 365 days.
- **NEXO**: revisit only if Nexo publishes a user-verifiable Proof of Reserves **and** holds its own
  MiCAR licence. Neither is true today.

### What not to do

- Don't average down on a thesis you haven't re-underwritten. "It's cheaper" is not new information.
- Don't chase yield with the sleeve (§5.2).
- Don't let a 5% sleeve consume 50% of your attention — the reason the equity mandate exists is that
  it compounds while you sleep.
- Don't buy NEXO because the dividend looks like a yield. It is funded by the loan book that is the
  risk.
